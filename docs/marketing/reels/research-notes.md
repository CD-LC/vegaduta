# How promo reels for a product like VegaDūta should look (research, 2026-10-21)

Sources read (vendor and creator blogs; directional, not measured data):
- SmartSites, "Best practices for high-performing Instagram Reels ads in 2026" – hook inside the first
  0.5–3 s; open on the problem or the outcome, not the logo.
- Blitzcut, "B2B and SaaS reel hooks for 2026" – your buyer is a tiny share of any feed, so the hook
  itself should name the role or situation ("if you run support…", "if you self-host…").
- quso.ai / SleekPost / prodshort, "Instagram Reels best practices 2026" – on-screen text for muted
  viewing, 1–3 hashtags, keep text out of the UI zones (bottom ~300 px and right ~150 px of a 1080×1920
  frame), first caption line carries the point.
- miraflow.ai / socialchamp / influenceflow, "YouTube Shorts best practices 2026" – 9:16 at 1080×1920,
  under 60 s (15–35 s sweet spot), reset attention every 2–4 s with a cut or motion, burned-in captions.
- Gillian Perkins, "What's working on YouTube in 2026" – Shorts convert to subscribers poorly on
  their own; treat them as a top-of-funnel test and point to a longer video.

What we applied:
1. Hook in ≤2.5 s, spoken and on screen, framed as a situation ("You just inherited the support
   queue") or a bold outcome ("One command. Your own agent platform.").
2. Problem → one real screen that fixes it → one receipt (a log line, an approval card, a number)
   → CTA. Every reel shows the real product, never a mock-up.
3. 25–45 s for Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts; a 75 s overview for YouTube and LinkedIn.
4. Captions burned in (big, high-contrast, 3–6 words per card, inside the safe zone); full SRT
   files shipped next to each video for platform captions.
5. Voice: one warm, clear narrator; music under the voice (ducked ~10 dB) and louder on the
   hook and the end card. Music is synthesized here, so there is nothing to license.
6. Each reel ends with the one-line install command and the GitHub path; no "link in bio"
   dependency.
7. 16:9 cuts for YouTube (long form and thumbnails) and LinkedIn, 1:1 covers for the grid.
