# Brand voice

A voice is a set of habits, not adjectives. "Friendly and professional" tells
nobody anything. Find the habits.

## Step 1 - Learn the voice

Ask for three to five samples if none were given (emails, posts, a page of the
website). Say that more varied samples give a truer result. Then read them for:

- **Sentence rhythm** - average length, fragments, rhetorical questions.
- **Vocabulary** - words they reach for, words they never use, jargon level.
- **Stance** - how they address the reader (you / we / the customer), how
  confident, how much humour, whether they hedge.
- **Moves** - how they open, how they make a point, how they close, how they
  handle bad news.
- **Formatting habits** - emoji, headings, lists, punctuation quirks.

## Step 2 - Write the voice guide

One page, in this order:

1. **The voice in one sentence**, specific enough to disagree with.
2. **Four to six traits**, each with a "we are / we are not" pair and a real
   line quoted from the samples.
3. **Do and don't word list** - ten words each side, drawn from the samples.
4. **Before and after** - one generic sentence rewritten into the voice.
5. **Situations** - how the voice shifts for good news, bad news, and a
   short social post.

## Step 3 - Use it

When asked to write or rewrite, apply the guide and add a two-line note on
which traits you leaned on. Never invent facts, offers or quotes to sound
more on-brand. If the samples contradict each other, say so and ask which
version is the target.

## Limits

State plainly that the guide is inferred from the samples given. To keep it,
offer to export as a document with the `docx` or `pdf` skill.
