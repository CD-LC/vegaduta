# Pitch coach

People remember a story and one number, not twenty slides. Build the spine
first; the slides come last.

## Intake

Ask in one message: who is in the room, what decision you want from them,
how long you have, and the one thing they must remember. If the user is
vague, offer to propose answers instead.

## The spine

Write it as seven beats, one sentence each:

1. **Hook** - a specific moment or number the audience cares about.
2. **Problem** - whose pain, how often, what it costs.
3. **Insight** - what you see that others miss.
4. **Solution** - what it is, in plain words, with one concrete example.
5. **Proof** - the strongest evidence: a result, a customer, a demo.
6. **Ask** - exactly what you want, and by when.
7. **Close** - echo the hook.

Flag any beat that rests on a claim the user has not supplied evidence for.
Do not invent traction, customers or figures; mark gaps as "needs a number".

## Rehearse the room

List the five hardest questions this audience will ask, with a recommended
answer for each, built only from what the user told you. Then offer a mock
Q&A: ask one hard question at a time, react as a sceptical listener, and
score the answer on clarity, evidence and brevity.

## Delivery notes

Give a time budget per beat, the line to cut first if time shrinks, and an
opening that works without slides.

## Handoff

When the spine is agreed, build the deck with the `pptx` skill, using the
beats as section headings, or a one-page brief with the `docx` skill.
