# Deck structures by purpose

Each numbered line is one slide (one `sections` entry). Adapt, merge or drop slides to fit the
material - never pad to hit a count.

## Investor / sales pitch (10-12 slides)

1. The problem - who has it and what it costs them today
2. Why now - what changed that makes this solvable or urgent
3. The solution - what it is, in one sentence, then 3 bullets
4. How it works - the mechanism, not a feature list
5. Proof - traction, pilots, results, named customers (only real ones)
6. Market - who buys, how many of them, how you reach them
7. Business model - who pays, how much, how often
8. Competition - honest alternatives, and the specific reason you win
9. Team - why these people
10. The ask - amount / next step, and what it unlocks

Sales variant: replace 6-9 with "What this looks like for you", "Implementation and timeline",
"Pricing".

## Status / board update (6-8 slides)

1. Headline - on track / at risk / off track, and the one reason why
2. Key metrics vs target (small table slide works well here)
3. What shipped since last update
4. What slipped, and the recovery plan
5. Risks and decisions needed - state each decision as a question with your recommendation
6. Next period - the 3 commitments

Lead with bad news. A status deck that hides the problem on slide 5 destroys trust.

## Training / explainer (8-12 slides)

1. What you will be able to do after this
2. Why it matters - the cost of getting it wrong
3. The core concept in one picture-in-words
4. Step 1 ... Step N - one slide per step, each ending with the common mistake
5. Worked example end to end
6. Quick reference - the checklist version
7. Where to get help

## Proposal / decision (5-7 slides)

1. The decision being asked for, and your recommendation
2. Background - only what the decider does not already know
3. Options considered (table: option, cost, benefit, risk)
4. Why the recommended option
5. What it costs and when it pays back
6. What happens next if approved
