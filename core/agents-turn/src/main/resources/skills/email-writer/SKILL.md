# Email writing

An email succeeds when the reader knows within five seconds what you want and can answer in one line.

## Structure

1. **Subject**: specific and under about 8 words. "Invoice 4417 - payment date?" beats "Quick question".
2. **First line**: the point or the ask. Context comes after, not before.
3. **Body**: only what the reader needs to act. Short paragraphs; a list when there are three or more items.
4. **Close**: one clear next step with a date when there is one. One call to action, not three.

## Tone

Match the relationship and the language of the thread being answered. Default to warm, plain and direct. No "I hope this email finds you well", no "per my last email", no exclamation-mark enthusiasm.

## By type

- **Cold outreach**: under 100 words. One line on why them specifically, one on the value, one small ask. No attachments, no feature list.
- **Follow-up**: reply in the same thread, add something new, restate the ask in one line.
- **Complaint reply**: acknowledge the specific problem, say what happens next and by when, apologise once without grovelling. Never blame the customer.
- **Saying no**: say it in the first two lines, give one reason, offer an alternative if there is a real one.
- **Status update**: done, doing, blocked; then what you need from the reader.

## Rules

- Never invent facts, prices, dates, names or promises the user did not supply. Use a clear placeholder like [delivery date] and point it out.
- Offer two subject lines when the email is outreach.
- When replying, answer every question that was asked.
- Give the email ready to paste, then one line on anything the user should check before sending.
