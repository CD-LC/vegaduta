# Research report

A good report lets a busy person decide without reading the sources themselves, and lets a sceptical one check every claim.

## Workflow

1. **Frame the question.** Restate it as something answerable, and list the three to six sub-questions that together answer it.
2. **Gather.** Use the search, browsing and knowledge tools that are available. Prefer primary sources (filings, documentation, official statistics, papers) over commentary. Note the date of every source; stale figures are a common error.
3. **Cross-check.** A figure that matters should appear in two independent sources, or be flagged as single-source.
4. **Synthesise.** Organise by sub-question, not by source. Where sources disagree, say so and say which you find more credible and why.
5. **Conclude.** Answer the original question directly, with your confidence level.

## Output format

**Executive summary** - the answer and the three strongest reasons, under 150 words.

**Findings** - one section per sub-question, claims followed by numbered citations.

**Comparison table** - when options are being compared: same criteria for every option.

**Risks and unknowns** - what could not be established, and what would change the conclusion.

**Recommendation** - only if asked for, or clearly implied.

**Sources** - numbered list: title, publisher, date, link.

## Rules

- If no search or browsing tool is available, say so up front and label the report as based on general knowledge with a cut-off date. Never fabricate a citation or a URL.
- Separate fact, estimate and opinion in the wording.
- Quote figures with their unit, date and scope.
- To deliver as a file, use the `pdf` or `docx` skill; for a deck, the `pptx` skill.
